You've got a loop in eight weeks, you've watched a few sample videos, and now there's a card in front of you. That's usually the moment this question gets typed. It's also the moment where most reviews are least useful, because a lot of them quote prices from 2023 that no longer exist on the purchase page.

So let's do the boring part first: what the platform costs today, what shows up after you pay, and the specific situations where the answer flips from "yes" to "no."

## What AlgoExpert costs right now

If you open the purchase page today, you don't get a menu of tiers. You get one card.

**Algo Expert — All Access: $119 for one year, against a $693 list price.** That single SKU bundles seven products: the core coding track, SystemsExpert, FrontendExpert, MLExpert, InfraExpert, iOSExpert, and BlockchainExpert.

That's a real change from the pricing most blog posts still describe. Older breakdowns (and there are dozens of them) talk about a $99 base tier, a $148 tier with system design, and a $199 full bundle. Those numbers circulated for a long time and some posts dated as recently as March 2026 still print them. They don't match what the vendor shows a new visitor today. Treat the $99/$148/$199 triplet as historical, not current.

Two things worth knowing about that $119:

- It sits under a flash-sale banner ("7 products for over 80% off") with a countdown timer attached. Observers have watched that timer tick down and reappear, so read $119 as a sale price with an unknown shelf life rather than a fixed rate card.
- Billing is annual only. There's no monthly option, no lifetime plan, and no free tier on the purchase page. Reviews consistently mention a roughly 30% loyalty discount if you renew, which would put a second year closer to the low $80s, but that's renewal pricing, not what you pay on day one.

## What you actually get after you pay

The core product is a curated question set with video explanations. Algo Expert's own product page says **200 coding interview questions**; a large number of reviews still say 160, which is where the number sat for years. Assume 200 and don't be surprised either way.

Each question comes with:

- A two-part video: conceptual walkthrough first, then a line-by-line code implementation
- Written solutions in nine languages (JavaScript, TypeScript, Python, Swift, Kotlin, C++, Java, C#, Go). The video walkthroughs themselves are in Python
- An in-browser workspace with test cases, hints, and a built-in timer so you can simulate a no-syntax-highlighting whiteboard round
- Space-time complexity analysis per problem
- A data structures crash course, four timed assessments, and a completion certificate
- Peer mock interviews, where you get matched with another subscriber in a shared editor

That's a coherent package. The gaps are the part the marketing sleeps on:

- **No free trial.** You can watch sample videos publicly, but you can't use the platform before paying.
- **Refund terms are restrictive.** Reviews across the board tell you to treat the purchase as final.
- **No company-specific question data.** No employer filters, no frequency-by-company view.
- **Thin coverage in a few heavily tested areas.** Multiple reviewers flag dynamic programming, advanced graphs, and niche structures like segment trees as underweight for FAANG-level loops.
- **No behavioral content.** Systems design exists inside the bundle, but the STAR-format, leadership-principles side of the loop is somebody else's product.

## The three cases where it's worth the money

Strip out the marketing and the decision comes down to how you learn and how long you have.

**1. You're starting from scratch and freeze in front of LeetCode's 3,000+ problem list.** Decision paralysis is a real cost. A fixed list with an intended order removes it. If you've never built a study plan and don't trust yourself to build one, a curated 200 with a suggested sequence is worth more than free access to an ocean.

**2. You learn by watching someone reason out loud.** This is AlgoExpert's actual moat. The videos are genuinely well produced and Clément walks through the intuition instead of jumping to the trick. If reading editorials makes your eyes glaze over, video is the format that keeps you moving.

**3. You have six to ten weeks and you need a syllabus, not a library.** In that window, a bundled year at $119 (against a struck-through $693) is cheap relative to the salary you're chasing, and you'll never hit the ceiling of the content.

## Where the math stops working

**You need volume to get fast, and 200 questions isn't volume.** A hard onsite loop rewards recognizing a pattern within a minute and coding it cleanly in fifteen. That comes from reps. Most people who take AlgoExpert seriously end up on LeetCode too, which means the real cost of an AlgoExpert-centered plan is the subscription plus LeetCode Premium (commonly quoted around $159/year or roughly $35/month) if you want company tags near the end. The bundle is a teaching layer, not the whole prep.

**You outgrow it inside a quarter.** If you finish the set in two or three months and don't touch the platform again for two years, you've rented a bootcamp, not bought a reference. With annual-only billing and no lifetime option, someone who interviews every couple of years keeps paying each cycle. Reviews cite that as the single most common regret.

**You interview two or three times a year.** At that cadence, a one-time purchase beats a recurring one every time you run the numbers, and there are platforms built specifically around that model.

## Where AlgoMonster is the weaker choice

It would be a strange review that only listed wins, so:

- **No video explanations at all.** If you learn best by watching, this is disqualifying. You'd be paying for a format that doesn't match how you study.
- **The problem bank is smaller.** The count is in the low hundreds against LeetCode's thousands. It's a teaching curriculum with practice attached, not a drilling platform.
- **System design stops short of senior loops.** Both AlgoExpert and AlgoMonster are secondary here if you're targeting L5+ and facing a dedicated design round. Budget separately.
- **Behavioral prep is thin.** Same story as AlgoExpert: format familiarity, not mastery.

## The decision, in practice

Answer three questions and stop deliberating.

1. **How soon is your loop?** Inside six to ten weeks and starting cold, the bundle-of-everything approach is the lower-effort path. Six months out, you have room to work through a pattern course first and drill after.
2. **Do you learn by watching or by reading?** This is the single biggest fork. Video, go AlgoExpert. Text and diagrams, go AlgoMonster. Neither is a quality judgment, it's a format match.
3. **How often do you interview?** Once every couple of years, a one-time purchase is the better math. Every year, an annual plan is fine.
